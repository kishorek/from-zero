Operators

One idea. An operator is a symbol that does something with values.

The idea

An operator sits between values, or just in front of one value, and produces a result. + adds. === asks a yes-or-no question. && combines two of those answers.

= is not an operator that compares. It stores a value in a variable. You already used it. Comparison uses ===.

SymbolNameExample
+ - * /Arithmetic10 + 2 is 12. 10 / 2 is 5.
%Remainder10 % 3 is 1. Three fits into ten three times, and one is left over.
=== !==Equality3 === 3 is true. 3 !== "3" is true, because a number and text are different kinds.
> < >= <=Comparison10 > 5 is true. 10 >= 10 is true.
&& || !Logic&& means both. || means either. ! flips true and false.

Why it matters

Every later decision is an operator. A condition is a comparison. A loop stops because a comparison becomes false. A price with tax is arithmetic. If the symbols are fuzzy, those lessons feel like spelling tricks.

A picture to hold

Arithmetic is a calculator. Comparison is a yes-or-no question on a form. && is two light switches in a row: both must be on. || is two switches side by side: either one is enough. ! is the switch that says the opposite of what it sees.

Line by line

  1. console.log(apples + 2);+ adds. 10 plus 2 is 12.
  2. console.log(apples - 2);- subtracts. 10 minus 2 is 8.
  3. console.log(apples * 2);* multiplies. The star is the multiply sign in code.
  4. console.log(apples / 2);/ divides. 10 divided by 2 is 5.
  5. console.log(apples % people);% is the remainder. 3 fits into 10 three times, and 1 apple is left.
  6. const enough = apples >= 5 && people !== 0;>= asks “at least 5?”. !== asks “not zero?”. && keeps the answer only when both are true.
  7. console.log(!enough);! flips the answer. enough is true, so !enough is false.

Try it yourself

7 cookies are shared by 3 friends. Print the remainder, then print whether there are cookies and there is at least one friend.

Practice. Remainder first. Then a yes-or-no answer.

Common mistakes

  • Writing = to ask “are these the same?”. One equals sign stores. === compares.
  • Using + on text and expecting a sum. "10" + 2 glues text and produces "102".
  • Reading && as the word “and” that you print. It produces true or false.

Recap

Arithmetic operators calculate. Comparison operators answer yes or no. &&, ||, and ! combine those answers. The next lessons use these symbols inside lists, records, and choices.
